## ESCALATION

### E-1 (Architect; AC-5(a) against AC-5(c), AC-8 and "ac_state wins"): a honoured `deferred` AC under the Withdrawn section

AC-5(a) reads: "An AC under the section has no entry, **or its entry is not a honoured `withdrawn` or `superseded`**: `… ac_state is <state>; it is live for coverage`."

Read literally, (a) also fires for a **honoured `deferred`** AC under the section. In that case:
- (c) already fires (`deferred ACs stay under Acceptance criteria`);
- (a)'s text, "it is live for coverage", is false. AC-8 exempts the AC, and the gate does exempt it, because "in every case the gate does what `ac_state` says".

So (a)'s literal scope contradicts its own message and AC-8.

**What the Developer did.** (a) fires only when the AC has no entry or its entry is not honoured (`index-specs.py:1494`). A honoured deferral under the section gets (c) alone. I think that is the sensible reading, but it departs from the letter of (a), so it is the Architect's call.

**Knock-on.** `template/specs/README.md:75` copies the literal wording: "an AC under the section with no honoured `withdrawn` or `superseded` entry is live for coverage". That is wrong for a honoured deferral, which is exempt. Whatever the Architect decides, the README needs to say the same thing.

**Recommended default:** keep the code.
- A DEC (new, or an item added to DEC-0029 while it is still proposed) restates (a) as "an AC under the section has no entry, or its entry is not honoured". `breaking: false`, because no implementer changes.
- In r2, the Developer rewords the README parenthetical to match.

My `test_labelled_acs_in_each_state_under_the_withdrawn_section` leaves this one line unpinned, so it stays green under either outcome.
